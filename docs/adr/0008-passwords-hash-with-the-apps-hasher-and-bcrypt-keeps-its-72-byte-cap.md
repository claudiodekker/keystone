# Passwords hash with the app's hasher, and bcrypt keeps its 72-byte cap

`keystone-password` hashes with Laravel's `Hash` under the app's own `config/hashing.php`, with no settings, pepper or pre-hash of its own, and core encrypts the hash before storing it. A stored hash is verified by the driver of its own algorithm (bcrypt, argon2i or argon2id, read with `password_get_info`), so hashes imported from another driver keep working with `HASH_VERIFY` on, and after a successful sign-in the password is rehashed under the app's driver and cost whenever `needsRehash` says so, unless the app turned `hashing.rehash_on_login` off. The proof carries a closure that makes the new hash; core calls it only once the account is signed in, so a barred account's correct password costs no more than a wrong one, and a failing hasher is reported without refusing the sign-in. Core writes the new hash only while the row still holds the hash that was verified, so a rehash never overwrites a password changed in the meantime and never moves the credential epoch.

Under bcrypt a new password is capped at 72 bytes, the most bcrypt reads, or at the app's lower `hashing.bcrypt.limit`; other drivers cap it at 1024 characters. We don't pre-hash to lift the cap: a pre-hash is a format of our own that imported Fortify hashes wouldn't match, and argon2id, which the installer offers, removes the cap outright. This is a deviation from ASVS 5.0 V6.2.9 (passwords of at least 64 characters allowed), since 72 bytes of non-ASCII text can be fewer than 64 characters.

## Consequences

- An imported bcrypt hash of a password longer than 72 bytes verifies against the password's first 72 bytes, as bcrypt always did (V6.2.8). Sign-in takes a password longer than 72 bytes for that reason, up to the 1024 characters every driver caps it at before hashing, since nothing else bounds the hashing cost of an argon2 check; only setting one is capped lower.
- An unknown account, or one without a password, is checked against a dummy hash made with the app's driver and cost, so it costs what a wrong password does. The dummy is made once and cached forever in the default cache store under a fingerprint of the whole hashing config, so changing the driver or cost makes a new one. With the `array` or `null` cache store it is made on every such request, which makes unknown accounts slower than known ones; production needs a persistent store.
- A wrong password against a hash of an older driver or cost takes that hash's time, not the dummy's, until the account's next sign-in rehashes it.
- The password package never trims `password`, `current_password` or `password_confirmation`, even when the app's `TrimStrings` drops Laravel's defaults, so surrounding spaces are part of the password at set and at sign-in.
